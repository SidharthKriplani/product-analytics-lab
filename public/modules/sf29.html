Chi-Square Test

Testing independence between categorical variables

You launch an experiment and notice that treatment got 52% of users instead of the intended 50%. Is that a problem or just noise? The tool you reach for is chi-square — but there are two distinct chi-square tests, and naming the right one matters. The goodness-of-fit test compares an observed split against an EXPECTED ratio you specify in advance — observed 52/48 versus the intended 50/50 — and that is exactly what SRM detection is. The test of independence is a different question: are two categorical variables related (e.g., does device type relate to conversion)? The 52/48 SRM case is goodness-of-fit, not independence — one categorical variable against a fixed expected ratio, not two variables against each other.

How this connects to experimentation: SRM (Sample Ratio Mismatch) detection is literally a chi-square test: observed vs expected assignment counts. Understanding chi-square makes SRM detection intuitive, not just a formula to run.

Key takeaways

  • Two distinct tests: goodness-of-fit (observed vs a fixed expected ratio) and independence (are two variables related?).
  • A 52/48 SRM check is goodness-of-fit — one variable against 50/50, not two variables against each other.
  • Naming the right one is half the skill.
