Instrumental Variables

Finding clean causal variation when treatment is endogenous

You want to know if using your premium feature causes long-term retention — but users who choose premium are already more engaged. You can't randomize who gets access, and observational data is hopeless here. The fix is to find something that nudges feature adoption without directly affecting retention: a random eligibility nudge, a UI lottery, a proximity-based push. That's an instrument. IV isolates the causal effect using only the variation in treatment driven by the instrument. Weak instruments inflate standard errors, and violations of the exclusion restriction silently bias estimates — you can't test this statistically.

How this connects to experimentation: IV is the last resort when randomization fails and other quasi-experiments don't apply. In product analytics, encouragement designs (randomly offering a feature) are a form of IV — intent-to-treat vs. TOT.

Key takeaways

  • An instrument nudges treatment adoption without directly affecting the outcome.
  • IV isolates the causal effect using only the variation driven by the instrument (a LATE).
  • Weak instruments inflate standard errors; exclusion-restriction violations bias silently and cannot be tested.
