Practical vs Statistical Significance

When significant is real but too small to matter — and the reverse

The experiment is significant — p = 0.001 — and the PM wants to ship. You pull up the raw numbers: the lift is 0.001% in conversion rate on a product that generates $100K/month. Annualized impact: $120. With 10M users, a 0.001% lift is statistically significant but worth nothing. With 1,000 users, a 5% lift might not reach significance yet. Effect size and business impact calculation must accompany every p-value.

How this connects to experimentation: The capstone concept: you now have all the tools — distributions, CLT, CI, hypothesis testing, power, MDE — to evaluate whether a result is both statistically trustworthy and practically worth acting on.

Key takeaways

  • With 10M users a 0.001% lift is significant but worth almost nothing; with 1,000 a real 5% may not reach significance.
  • A p-value tells you an effect exists; effect size tells you it matters.
  • Attach effect size and business impact to every p-value.
