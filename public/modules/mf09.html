Funnel Metrics

Measuring where users drop off and why it matters

Overall checkout conversion is 3.2% — down from 3.8% last quarter. Where do you even start? The aggregate tells you nothing actionable. But when you break it step by step — landing page to cart (flat), cart to payment entry (down 18%), payment entry to confirmation (flat) — the problem is obviously the payment step, not the product or the acquisition funnel. The absolute end-to-end rate matters far less than the relative drop between adjacent steps, which tells you exactly which surface needs attention.

How this connects to experimentation: Funnel metrics anchor experiment success criteria: a 2% lift in checkout conversion is meaningless without knowing which funnel step you moved. Running an A/B test on the full funnel blurs signal — test at the step level and roll up to end conversion.

Key takeaways

  • The aggregate rate tells you nothing — the relative drop between adjacent steps points to the surface.
  • Break checkout step by step; an 18% cart→payment drop is the target, not acquisition.
  • Test at the step level and roll up; testing the whole funnel blurs the signal.
