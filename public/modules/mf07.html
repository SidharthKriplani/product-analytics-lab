Designing a North Star Metric

What a North Star must do — and the traps to avoid

A new VP asks you to defend your team's North Star metric. You say "revenue." They push back: "That's what we extract, not what users get. What value do we deliver?" It stops you cold. A good North Star captures value delivered to users, not value extracted by the company. DAU measures engagement but not value. "Messages sent" for WhatsApp captures a behaviour that strongly predicts user value. Revenue is important — but it's a lagging extraction metric, not a North Star.

How this connects to experimentation: North Stars fail in experiments because they are too stable week-over-week to detect short-term treatment effects. You use them for long-run holdouts or to validate that your L1/L2 metrics are actually correlated with North Star improvement over cohort data.

Key takeaways

  • A North Star captures value delivered to users, not value extracted by the company.
  • "Messages sent" predicts user value; revenue is a lagging extraction metric, not a North Star.
  • Too stable week-over-week for experiments — use it for long-run holdouts and validating L1/L2 links.
