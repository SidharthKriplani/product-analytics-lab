ANOVA

Comparing means across three or more groups simultaneously

You're testing three checkout flow variants simultaneously. A colleague suggests running three separate t-tests to compare them. You know that's wrong — but can you explain why? Running three t-tests inflates your false positive rate to roughly 14%. ANOVA tests whether the variance between group means is larger than the variance within groups (the F-statistic) and controls the family-wise error rate for the omnibus test. Post-hoc pairwise tests (Tukey, Bonferroni) are then required to identify which specific groups differ.

How this connects to experimentation: In A/B testing, ANOVA is used when you have 3+ variants simultaneously. The F-test answers "is there any difference?" — post-hoc tests answer "which pairs differ?" Running 3 t-tests on a 3-variant experiment inflates false positive rate to 14%.

Key takeaways

  • Three t-tests on three variants inflate the false positive rate to ~14%.
  • ANOVA's F-test asks "is there any difference?" while controlling family-wise error.
  • Post-hoc tests (Tukey, Bonferroni) then identify which specific pairs differ.
