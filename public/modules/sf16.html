Skewness & Log-Normal Distributions

Why product metrics lie when you assume normality

The experiment shows a statistically significant lift in average revenue — but when you look at the raw data, three users in treatment each spent $10,000 and everyone else spent $12. Your "significant" result is three people. Revenue, session length, and LTV are right-skewed; the mean is dragged by the top 1%. When your metric is log-normal, median is more honest than mean, and log-transforming before A/B testing gives cleaner, more stable results statistically. The catch: a t-test on log(revenue) is testing the geometric mean (close to a median-like center), not mean revenue — so confirm that geometric-mean lift is still the number leadership actually cares about. If the business KPI is total or average dollars, a cleaner log result can answer a different question than the one you were asked.

How this connects to experimentation: This directly affects how you run and interpret A/B tests on revenue metrics. A significant mean lift might be driven by 3 whale users. A non-significant result might mask a real median lift.

Key takeaways

  • Revenue, session length, and LTV are right-skewed — the mean is dragged by the top 1%.
  • A "significant" mean lift can be three whale users; median is more honest.
  • Log-transform gives cleaner tests, but it tests the geometric mean — confirm that is the KPI leadership wants.
