Decompose Before You Diagnose

Why the first step is always breaking the metric apart

You're in a war-room call and someone says "DAU is down 20%, must be the onboarding change we shipped." Stop. A 20% DAU drop can be new user acquisition failure, D1 retention collapse, or resurrected user dropoff — three completely different fixes. Decompose first, diagnose second. Jumping to a cause before breaking the metric apart is the single most common RCA mistake.

How this connects to experimentation: In A/B test analysis, decomposition tells you whether a primary metric movement was driven by the target segment or a composition shift. The decomposition skill transfers directly from RCA to experiment evaluation.

Key takeaways

  • One aggregate drop can hide three unrelated causes: new-user acquisition, retention, or resurrection.
  • Decompose first, diagnose second — break the metric apart before you name a cause.
  • Jumping to a cause before decomposing is the single most common RCA mistake.
