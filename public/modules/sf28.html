Bootstrap Resampling

Simulation-based inference when parametric assumptions break down

You're running an A/B test on revenue per user. The distribution has a long right tail, only 800 users in each group, and your data scientist says "we can't use a t-test here — CLT won't save us at this sample size." Bootstrap is one tool to reach for. Bootstrap builds a sampling distribution by resampling with replacement from your observed data — no normality assumption required. Run 1000+ resamples, compute your statistic each time, and the distribution of those statistics is your bootstrap sampling distribution. But be honest about its limits: bootstrap relaxes the normality assumption, it does not invent information the sample never contained. With very heavy tails and small n — exactly the 800-user, whale-driven revenue case — the rare extreme values may simply be absent from your data, so the bootstrap can be just as unreliable as the t-test. It is a sharper tool, not a guaranteed rescue; collecting more data or capping/transforming the metric may matter more.

How this connects to experimentation: Bootstrap CIs are used in A/B testing when the metric distribution is skewed (revenue, LTV, session length) and the CLT approximation is unreliable. Many tech companies use bootstrap as a default for revenue metrics rather than t-tests — but with heavy tails and small samples, no resampling method can fix a sample that is too small to have captured the tail.

Key takeaways

  • Bootstrap builds a sampling distribution by resampling with replacement — no normality assumption.
  • Run 1000+ resamples, compute the statistic each time, read off the distribution.
  • Honest limit: with heavy tails and small n, the tail may be absent from the data — bootstrap cannot invent it.
