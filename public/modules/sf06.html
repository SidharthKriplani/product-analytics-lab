Areas Under the Curve

Turning z-scores into probabilities

The experiment result is in and the engineer says "the z-score is 2.1 — so we're good, right?" You need to know what that number actually translates to before you call it significant. The area under the normal curve between two z-scores is the probability a random draw falls between them — and that's literally what p-values are: tail area beyond your test statistic. A two-sided p-value is the area in BOTH tails beyond +|z| and -|z| (so for z = 2.1, both the area above +2.1 and below -2.1); a one-tailed p-value counts just the single tail in the hypothesized direction.

How this connects to experimentation: P-values are tail areas under the null distribution beyond your test statistic — both tails for a two-sided test, one tail for a one-sided test.

Key takeaways

  • Area under the curve between two z-scores = the probability a draw falls between them.
  • p-values are tail area beyond your test statistic.
  • Two-sided counts both tails (±|z|); one-tailed counts a single tail in the hypothesized direction.
