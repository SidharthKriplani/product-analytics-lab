Leading vs Lagging Indicators

Proxies for future health vs confirmation of past outcomes

Your CEO wants to know if the new onboarding flow is working. Revenue won't tell you for 90 days, so you reach for D7 retention as the early read. But pause on the leap everyone makes here: D7 retention is only a trustworthy proxy if you have actually measured, on historical cohorts, that D7 retention moves LTV and churn. For a brand-new flow that link is assumed, not proven — you are betting that a relationship which held for the old experience also holds for the new one. Revenue confirms what already happened; a leading indicator only tells you what is about to happen if its link to the outcome is real. The core skill (and the core risk) is picking a proxy whose connection to the lagging metric you have validated, not just one that feels predictive.

How this connects to experimentation: A/B tests need fast-moving, sensitive leading indicators as primary metrics — you can't wait 90 days to see LTV move. But you report results in terms of the lagging metric (revenue, subscriber count) to make the business case. The whole approach rests on one validated assumption: that the leading metric actually predicts the lagging outcome. Establish that link on past cohort data first; if you can't, you are flying on a proxy you only hope is predictive.

Key takeaways

  • A leading indicator is trustworthy only if you have measured its link to the outcome on past cohorts.
  • For a brand-new flow that link is assumed, not proven — you are betting an old relationship still holds.
  • Lagging metrics confirm the past; leading metrics predict the future only if the link is real.
