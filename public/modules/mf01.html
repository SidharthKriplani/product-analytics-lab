The Metrics Hierarchy

North Star, L1, L2, and Guardrails — how metrics nest

A PM asks you to build a metrics dashboard for next quarter's OKR review and you're staring at 40 possible metrics with no idea which ones belong where. Every metric in your stack answers a different question: the North Star asks "are we winning?", L1s ask "why?", L2s ask "where specifically?", and guardrails ask "are we breaking anything while we chase the win?" Without this hierarchy, every metric looks equally important — which means nothing gets acted on.

How this connects to experimentation: When you design an A/B test, you pick a primary metric (usually L1 or L2) and guardrail metrics. The North Star is too slow-moving to use as a primary test metric — but it is what you ultimately care about.

Key takeaways

  • Every metric answers a different question: North Star ("are we winning?"), L1 ("why?"), L2 ("where?"), guardrails ("what are we breaking?").
  • Without the hierarchy every metric looks equally important — so nothing gets acted on.
  • The North Star is what you ultimately care about, but too slow to use as a test's primary metric.
