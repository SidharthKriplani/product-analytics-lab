Multi-Level RCA

Navigating investigations where multiple root causes interact across layers

You find a logging bug that explains 40% of the drop. You report it and the team ships a fix — but the metric recovers only partially. There was a second cause underneath the first: a real retention regression that the data quality issue had been obscuring. Real incidents often have two or three interacting causes. Stop at the first plausible explanation and you write an incomplete RCA. The test: remove each cause individually and ask — would the full drop still exist?

How this connects to experimentation: Multi-level causal thinking is the senior skill in both RCA and experiment analysis. When a primary metric moves unexpectedly in an A/B test, the cause is often a combination of an interaction effect, a novelty effect, and a segment composition shift — not a single clean explanation. Stopping at the first plausible cause is the most common senior analyst failure mode.

Key takeaways

  • Real incidents often stack two or three interacting causes on top of each other.
  • A data-quality issue can obscure a real regression underneath — a partial recovery after the fix is the tell.
  • Test each cause: remove it individually — would the full drop still exist?
