Counter Metrics and Guardrails

What to protect when optimising for a primary metric

The notifications team shows you a 12% lift in daily opens. Great — until someone pulls up app store reviews and sees a spike in "too many notifications" complaints. The lift was real; it was just bought by degrading something you cared about more. That is what a counter metric protects against: almost every primary metric has a partner it can be inflated at the expense of. Push CTR and you can crush satisfaction. Push speed of checkout and you can hide a rise in fraud. Push session count and you can quietly grow rage-quits. A counter metric names the thing the primary can cannibalise, so a "win" that is really a quality tradeoff shows up as a win on one number and a loss on the other — instead of as a celebration.

How this connects to experimentation: For every primary metric in an A/B test, ask what it could be gamed against and pair it with the opposing counter metric. The primary tells you the change worked; the counter metric tells you what it cost. (How you enforce the counter metric in the ship decision — pre-committing it as a hard block — is its own discipline, covered under guardrails.)

Key takeaways

  • Almost every primary metric has a partner it can be inflated at the expense of.
  • Push CTR → crush satisfaction; push checkout speed → hide fraud; push sessions → grow rage-quits.
  • A counter metric names that partner, so a quality tradeoff shows as a win on one number, loss on the other.
