When a Flat Rate Is the Lie

A ratio that doesn't move can hide a numerator and denominator both collapsing

Session-to-purchase rate has been flat for three months. The PM declares the checkout flow stable and moves on. Then someone notices absolute purchases are down 20%. How is the rate flat? Because sessions fell by the same proportion — the numerator and denominator collapsed together and the ratio held steady on top of two failing businesses. This is the mirror image of a ratio that moves while every segment is flat: here the ratio sits perfectly still while both of its parts are on fire. A stable rate is not evidence of a stable system. Whenever a ratio refuses to move, pull the raw numerator and denominator counts separately before you conclude that nothing is happening.

How this connects to experimentation: In A/B tests, ratio metrics are preferred as primary metrics because they normalise for exposure. But if the treatment changes who enters the experiment (denominator selection bias), the ratio is biased. SRM checks catch the population problem; decomposition catches the mix problem.

Key takeaways

  • A ratio can sit perfectly still while numerator and denominator both collapse together.
  • A stable rate is not evidence of a stable system — it can rest on two failing businesses.
  • Whenever a ratio refuses to move, pull the raw numerator and denominator counts separately.
