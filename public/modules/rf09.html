Seasonality and Trend Separation

Decomposing a time series into trend, seasonality, and residual signal

You're reviewing the weekly metrics report and DAU is down 12% week-over-week. The PM is alarmed. You pull up the same week last year — also down 12%. That one match does not prove the product is fine; it raises the seasonality hypothesis. Before you close the case, confirm it: does the same dip show up across two or three prior years, or against a seasonally-adjusted baseline? One coincident year could itself be a fluke. Analysts who only look at WoW changes without a seasonality-adjusted baseline generate false alarms every single quarter. Same-week-last-year is the first sanity check you run, not the last.

How this connects to experimentation: Seasonality-adjusted baselines are equally critical in A/B test analysis. An experiment spanning a holiday must compare treatment and control within the same time window — and any WoW metric movement in the results should be checked against the same week in prior years before being attributed to the treatment.

Key takeaways

  • Same-week-last-year is the first sanity check, not the last — one matching year could itself be a fluke.
  • Confirm the seasonality hypothesis against two or three prior years or a seasonally-adjusted baseline before closing.
  • WoW changes read without a seasonal baseline generate false alarms every quarter.
