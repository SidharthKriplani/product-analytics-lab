Ratio Metrics and Their Traps

Why rates and percentages mislead — and how to fix them

You present the monthly metrics review: conversion rate is up 2%. Leadership is pleased. Then a PM asks how it looks by traffic source — and in every single channel, conversion is flat or down. How is the aggregate up? A new high-intent organic channel doubled in volume, pulling up the mix. Any ratio can move in a direction that looks good overall while moving in the opposite direction in every segment — this is what happens when the mix shifts. Always decompose a ratio into numerator movement, denominator movement, and mix shift before concluding anything.

How this connects to experimentation: Conversion rate is the classic A/B test primary metric — but if your treatment changes who enters the funnel (denominator), it can inflate CVR without any real improvement. Always check both the rate and absolute counts.

Key takeaways

  • Any ratio can rise in aggregate while falling in every segment — a mix shift did it.
  • Always decompose a ratio into numerator, denominator, and mix before concluding anything.
  • If treatment changes who enters the funnel, CVR can inflate with no real gain — check absolute counts too.
