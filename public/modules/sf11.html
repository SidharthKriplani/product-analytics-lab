Hypothesis Testing

Making decisions under uncertainty

The experiment readout shows p = 0.03 and your PM asks "Does this mean there's a 97% chance the feature works?" You pause — because that's completely wrong. A p-value answers: "If there were no real effect, how likely is it that we'd see a result this extreme or more extreme?" Small p means the data is hard to explain if nothing is happening — it does not tell you the probability the feature works.

How this connects to experimentation: Experiment decisions hinge on p < α. Understanding what p-value actually measures prevents the most common experiment interpretation mistakes.

Key takeaways

  • A p-value = "if there were no effect, how likely is data this extreme?" — not the probability the feature works.
  • Small p means the data is hard to explain under the null.
  • Decisions hinge on p < α; misreading p is the classic experiment mistake.
