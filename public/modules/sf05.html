Z-Scores

Standardizing — where does a value sit?

You have two metrics from different experiments — one in dollars, one in percentage points — and you need to compare which result is more unusual. Raw numbers can't answer that. A z-score answers "how many standard deviations from the mean is this value?" and puts values on a common, scale-free footing. One caveat: a z-score standardizes scale, but the percentile it maps to depends on the distribution shape — a z of 2 is the ~98th percentile under a normal, but not under a skewed distribution.

How this connects to experimentation: The test statistic in a t-test is essentially a z-score: (observed effect - expected under null) / standard error.

Key takeaways

  • A z-score answers "how many SDs from the mean?" — putting different-scale values on common footing.
  • Caveat: a z standardizes scale, but the percentile depends on distribution shape (z=2 ≈ 98th only under a normal).
  • A t-test statistic is essentially a z-score: (effect − null) / standard error.
