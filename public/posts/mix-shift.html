Mix Shift: The Hidden Cause Behind Half Your Metric Movements

Revenue grew but margin fell. CVR improved but GMV didn't. Mix shift causes movements that look wrong until you understand the composition change underneath.

Mix shift is a change in the composition of your population, not a change in behavior. When the composition changes, aggregate metrics move — even if nothing about individual users, sellers, or products actually changed. Mix shift is responsible for roughly half of the metric movements that analysts initially misdiagnose as behavioral changes. It's not that something broke or improved. It's that who you're measuring changed.

The Shift-Share Decomposition

The formal way to diagnose mix shift is the shift-share decomposition. Any change in an aggregate metric equals the sum of two components: the within-segment change (did each segment's rate actually change?) and the composition change (did the mix of segments shift?). If the aggregate moved but every segment's rate held flat, the entire movement is composition — pure mix shift. If segments moved and the composition held flat, it's pure behavioral change. Most real situations are some combination of both, and the decomposition tells you how much of each.

Crafted marketplace: GMV per seller up 8%, but not what it looks like: Last month vs this month:
- Overall GMV per active seller: $1,190 → $1,285 (+8%)

First instinct: sellers are getting more productive. But decompose by segment:
- SMB sellers (avg $820/month): flat at $820
- Mid-market sellers (avg $2,400/month): flat at $2,390
- Enterprise sellers (avg $12,000/month): flat at $12,050

Not a single segment moved meaningfully. Now check the mix:
- SMB sellers: 78% of active sellers last month → 68% this month
- Enterprise sellers: 5% last month → 12% this month

The aggregate GMV per seller went up 8% entirely because the enterprise segment grew as a share of the base. No individual seller became more productive. This is mix shift, not improvement. The right story for the PM: "Our enterprise cohort is growing faster than SMB, which is pulling up the average. Seller productivity within each tier is flat."
How to identify mix shift vs real signal: Run the shift-share decomposition: 1. Compute the metric for each segment separately for both periods. 2. If segment rates moved: behavioral change is present. 3. If segment rates held flat but the aggregate moved: pure mix shift. 4. To quantify: what would the aggregate be if the mix were the same as last period? Compute this counterfactual and subtract from the actual aggregate. The difference is the mix shift contribution.

Mix shift also explains why A/B test results can look different at the aggregate level vs the segment level. If your treatment disproportionately reached enterprise users (higher converters), aggregate CVR in treatment will look elevated even if the treatment had zero causal effect on anyone. This is why segmenting A/B test readouts by user type is as important as checking the aggregate result.
